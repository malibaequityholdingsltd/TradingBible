> **Status.** Draft policy and acknowledgment. It applies to founders, directors, officers, employees, contractors and immediate family members who wish to participate in the round. It does not approve any participation.

## 1. Purpose

The company may raise capital from investors who are also founders, directors, officers, employees or affiliates. Those purchases can be legitimate, but they create conflicts, disclosure obligations and the risk that insiders receive better terms or information than outside investors. This policy establishes a single standard: **insiders invest on the same terms as outside investors, with written conflict approval and no preferential information.**

## 2. Covered persons

This policy applies to:

- each director and officer of the company;
- each founder and holder of more than 5% of the company’s voting securities;
- each employee or contractor who has access to offering terms, investor information or the cap table; and
- each immediate family member or controlled entity of the above.

## 3. Same-terms rule

A covered person may purchase SAFEs in the offering only:

1. on the same price, valuation cap, discount, minimum, pro rata rights and closing terms as unrelated investors;
2. under the same form of SAFE and the same disclosure package;
3. after completing the same eligibility, bad-actor and risk documentation;
4. without any side letter, verbal promise, allocation preference, information right or fee arrangement that is not disclosed to all investors; and
5. after the disinterested directors approve the participation in writing.

## 4. Conflict disclosure

Before a covered person may subscribe, they must deliver a written disclosure to the board stating:

- their relationship to the company;
- the amount they propose to invest;
- the source of funds;
- any existing loan, guarantee, royalty, consulting or other arrangement with the company;
- any discussion they have had with an investor about the offering; and
- any other fact that could reasonably affect the board’s judgment.

The disclosure must be recorded in the board minutes and retained with the closing records.

## 5. Information barriers

A covered person who participates in the round may not:

- receive information about other investors’ allocations, pricing, negotiations or closing status that is not available to all investors;
- use their position to obtain earlier access to the offering documents;
- influence the allocation process for their own benefit;
- trade in the company’s securities while in possession of material non-public information about the offering; or
- disclose offering information to a person who is not cleared to receive it.

## 6. Board approval

The disinterested directors must approve each insider participation by resolution. If all directors are interested, the company must obtain an independent fairness review or counsel-approved alternative before accepting the subscription. The approval must state:

- the amount approved;
- the terms approved;
- the basis for concluding that the terms are no more favorable than those available to outside investors; and
- any conditions, including a cap on the insider’s allocation.

## 7. No preferential allocation

Insider participation may not reduce the amount available to outside investors below the board-approved minimum or cause the company to exceed the Maximum Offering Amount. If the offering is oversubscribed, insiders must be treated on the same allocation basis as outside investors, unless the board discloses and approves a different basis in advance.

## 8. Prohibited conduct

No covered person may:

- offer to sell securities to another person in exchange for a fee or commission;
- promise an investor a return, a board seat, a advisory role or a future allocation in exchange for a subscription;
- use company funds to purchase SAFEs;
- cause the company to guarantee an investor’s investment;
- accept a loan from an investor and treat it as a SAFE purchase; or
- sign a SAFE on behalf of the company if they are the investor or an affiliate of the investor.

## 9. Records

The company shall retain:

- each insider disclosure;
- each board conflict approval;
- each insider subscription and SAFE;
- each wire and closing record; and
- each communication about the insider’s participation.
