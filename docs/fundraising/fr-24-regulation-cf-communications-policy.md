> **Internal policy.** This policy applies to every director, officer, employee, contractor, agency and agent who communicates about a possible or active Regulation CF offering. It must be read with the qualified offering statement and the intermediary’s rules. It does not authorize any communication that counsel has not approved.

## 1. Core rule

A Regulation CF offering is public only after the SEC qualifies the offering statement and the registered intermediary opens the campaign. Before that point, the company must not offer securities, solicit investments, accept funds or describe the offering as live, open, approved or available.

## 2. Pre-qualification phase

During the pre-qualification phase:

1. The company may prepare the Form C, financial statements and campaign materials internally.
2. The company may communicate with the intermediary, counsel, accountants and service providers under confidentiality.
3. The company may not publish a campaign page, “invest now” button, waitlist, reservation, allocation request or payment link.
4. The company may not send a mass email, social-media post, press release or public webinar about the offering.
5. If counsel approves a limited testing-the-waters or educational communication, the company must use the exact approved script and must state that no offer is being made.
6. Every permitted communication must be recorded in the communications register.

## 3. Live-campaign phase

After qualification and the intermediary’s launch:

1. The company may communicate only through the qualified offering statement, the intermediary’s campaign page and approved campaign materials.
2. The company must not make a statement that is inconsistent with the qualified offering statement.
3. The company must not promise a return, a valuation, a listing, a dividend or a liquidity event.
4. The company must not describe the securities as “safe,” “guaranteed,” “approved” or “pre-vetted.”
5. The company must direct investors to the intermediary’s page for subscription, payment and cancellation.
6. The company must not accept funds directly from an investor.
7. The company must not use testimonials, endorsements or influencer content unless the intermediary and counsel approve it and the required disclosures are included.
8. The company must correct any material misstatement promptly and in the same channel.

## 4. Social media and public statements

The company’s official accounts may share the intermediary’s campaign page only after qualification. Any post must:

- identify the offering as a Regulation CF offering;
- link to the qualified offering statement;
- include the required risk warning;
- avoid projections, price targets and return claims; and
- be approved by the communications owner and counsel before publication.

Employees and contractors may not post about the offering from personal accounts unless they use approved language and disclose their relationship to the company. The company should adopt a short social-media policy for the campaign period.

## 5. Email and SMS

1. Marketing emails and texts require prior consent and an unsubscribe or opt-out mechanism.
2. Transactional messages about an investor’s own subscription may be sent without marketing consent, but must still be accurate and retained.
3. No email or text may contain full bank details, passwords, one-time codes or private keys.
4. No fundraising email may be sent to a purchased list or an unrestricted audience.
5. Every fundraising email must identify the company, the offering and the intermediary, and must link to the qualified offering statement after qualification.
6. SMS messages must include the company name and an opt-out instruction where required.

## 6. Recordkeeping

The company must retain:

- the exact content of each communication;
- the date, time, channel and audience;
- the person who approved it;
- the source of any data or claim;
- any correction, retraction or complaint; and
- the legal basis for the communication.

Records must be retained for at least the period required by law and the intermediary’s agreement, and must be available to counsel and regulators on request.

## 7. Complaints and corrections

Any complaint about an offering communication must be:

1. logged within one business day;
2. acknowledged within two business days;
3. reviewed by the communications owner and counsel;
4. corrected in the same channel if the complaint is substantiated; and
5. reported to the intermediary if required.

## 8. Prohibited statements

The following statements are prohibited unless counsel has approved the exact language and the qualified offering statement supports it:

- “SEC approved” or “SEC cleared”;
- “guaranteed return” or “risk-free”;
- “IPO coming” or “will list”;
- “valuation will be” or “price will be”;
- “invest now” before qualification;
- “limited allocation” or “closing soon” before the campaign opens;
- “our token will” or “TBC will”;
- “our fund will” or “TradingBible Capital will”;
- any revenue, subscriber, AUM or performance figure that is not in the qualified offering statement; and
- any statement that an investor will receive a board seat, advisory role or future allocation in exchange for investing.

## 9. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Chief executive officer | Final approval of campaign strategy and material communications |
| Communications owner | Maintains the register, approves routine posts and manages complaints |
| Counsel | Reviews regulated statements, testing-the-waters materials and corrections |
| Intermediary | Reviews campaign page, investor limits, escrow and required disclosures |
| Finance owner | Confirms use-of-proceeds and financial figures |
| All personnel | Comply with this policy and escalate uncertain statements |

## 10. Training and certification

Every person who will communicate about the offering must complete a training session before the campaign and certify compliance. The training must cover the pre-qualification prohibition, the qualified offering statement, the investor-limit rules, the complaint process and the recordkeeping requirements.
