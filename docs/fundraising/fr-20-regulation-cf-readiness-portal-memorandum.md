> **Internal memorandum.** This document is a preparation record, not an offer, a solicitation, a Form C filing or evidence of SEC qualification. No Regulation CF campaign may begin until the SEC qualifies the offering statement and a registered intermediary opens the campaign.

## 1. Purpose

This memorandum records the work required before TradingBible Holdings, Inc. can decide whether to conduct a Regulation CF offering. It is intended for the board, counsel and the finance owner. It does not authorize a campaign.

## 2. What Regulation CF requires

A Regulation CF offering generally involves:

1. an issuer that is organized in and operating in the United States;
2. a offering made through a FINRA-registered funding portal or broker-dealer;
3. an offering statement on Form C, including the required disclosures and financial statements;
4. SEC qualification of the offering statement before the offering begins;
5. investor-limit controls administered by the intermediary;
6. escrow or other compliant handling of investor funds;
7. ongoing annual and current reporting after the offering; and
8. recordkeeping and communications controls.

The company must confirm the current rules, forms, dollar limits and financial-statement requirements with counsel. The rules can change, and the company’s eligibility depends on facts that must be verified.

## 4. Portal selection criteria

The company should evaluate at least three intermediaries, if available, on the following:

- current FINRA registration and disciplinary history;
- Regulation CF campaign experience and completion rate;
- quality of disclosure review and issuer support;
- escrow, investor-limit and payment controls;
- fees, expenses and conflicts;
- data security, privacy and retention;
- communications and social-media rules;
- ongoing reporting support;
- termination, transfer and records-return terms; and
- references from issuers and counsel.

The company should not select a portal solely on price or on a promise of a faster launch.

## 5. Financial statement preparation

The company should engage an independent accountant early. The level of financial-statement review or audit required depends on the offering amount and the company’s prior sales. The company should not:

- use management accounts as if they were audited;
- omit related-party transactions;
- net revenue against expenses without disclosure;
- describe a working version as “final” or “approved”; or
- use a projection as a historical result.

## 6. Disclosure preparation

The Form C narrative should cover:

- the company’s business, products and stage;
- the risks that could cause failure;
- the use of proceeds and budget assumptions;
- management, ownership and related-party relationships;
- outstanding debt, SAFEs, options and convertible instruments;
- material litigation, regulatory and tax matters;
- the terms of the securities and resale restrictions;
- the investor-limit rules; and
- the company’s reporting obligations.

Every material statement should be traceable to a company record. The disclosure should be written in plain language and should not rely on marketing claims.

## 7. Communications rules

Before qualification, the company may communicate only in the manner permitted by counsel and the intermediary. Any permitted communication must:

1. identify the company;
2. state that no offer is being made;
3. state that any offer will be made only through the registered intermediary after qualification;
4. avoid amount, price, valuation, allocation or return statements;
5. avoid directing anyone to send money; and
6. be recorded in the communications register.

After qualification, the company must use the qualified offering statement and the intermediary’s campaign page. It must not make statements that are inconsistent with the offering statement.

## 10. Decision gates

- [ ] Board authorizes preparation only (FR-02).
- [ ] Portal selected and engagement letter signed.
- [ ] Accountant engaged and financial statements prepared.
- [ ] Form C prepared and reviewed by counsel.
- [ ] Communications policy adopted.
- [ ] Investor-limit and escrow controls confirmed.
- [ ] Board approves filing the offering statement.
- [ ] SEC qualifies the offering statement.
- [ ] Campaign opens through the registered intermediary.

## 11. Recommendation

The company should not launch a Regulation CF campaign until the disclosure, financial statements, portal controls and ongoing reporting capability are ready. A premature campaign creates regulatory, reputational and investor-relations risk that outweighs any speed advantage.
