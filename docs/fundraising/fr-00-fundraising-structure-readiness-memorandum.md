## 1. Purpose

TradingBible Holdings, Inc. is considering a financing to support product delivery, operating costs and the public launch programme. This memorandum compares the two structures that can realistically be prepared from the company’s current record:

1. a private placement to accredited U.S. investors under Rule 506(b) of Regulation D, together with an offshore tranche under Regulation S; and
2. a future Regulation CF offering through a FINRA-registered funding portal, if the company, its disclosures and its financial records are ready.

The company should not run both tracks at the same time without a written allocation policy. A public crowdfunding campaign and a quiet private placement create different disclosure, solicitation and recordkeeping obligations. Mixing them casually is a compliance defect, not a marketing choice.

## 3. Recommended structure

The recommended first step is a private placement of post-money SAFE instruments, subject to the following conditions:

- the board adopts a written resolution approving the form of SAFE, the maximum aggregate purchase price, the minimum investment, the valuation cap, the use-of-proceeds budget and the officer authority;
- offers are made only to persons with whom the company has a pre-existing substantive relationship, and only to accredited U.S. investors or eligible non-U.S. investors;
- there is no general solicitation, public advertisement, social-media campaign, mass email, webinar open to the public or press release announcing the offering;
- every investor completes the accredited-investor or Regulation S certificate, the bad-actor questionnaire and the risk acknowledgment before signing;
- the company files Form D within 15 calendar days after the first sale and makes any required state notice filings;
- DocuSign is used for execution, but only after the signer’s identity and capacity are verified; and
- no funds are accepted until the company has verified the investor, the subscription documents and the funding instructions.

A Regulation CF offering should be treated as a separate, later decision. It requires a FINRA-registered intermediary, a Form C offering statement, SEC qualification, a portal account, investor-limit controls, escrow arrangements and ongoing reporting. It is not a shortcut around the private-placement gates.

## 5. Private placement mechanics

### 5.1 Rule 506(b)

Rule 506(b) permits an issuer to sell securities to an unlimited number of accredited investors and to up to 35 non-accredited investors, provided that the issuer does not use general solicitation or general advertising and that each non-accredited investor receives the required disclosure. The company should not rely on the 35 non-accredited investor allowance for this round unless counsel confirms the disclosure package and the investor’s sophistication. The practical standard is: accredited investors only in the United States.

The company must have a reasonable belief that each U.S. purchaser is accredited. A self-certification questionnaire is evidence, but it is not a substitute for a reasonable verification process where the facts call for one. The company should retain the questionnaire, the investor’s supporting information and the basis for its conclusion.

### 5.2 Regulation S

Regulation S is available for offers and sales outside the United States that are made in an offshore transaction and are not directed selling efforts into the United States. The company should use it only for investors who are not U.S. persons and who provide a completed Regulation S certificate. A non-U.S. mailing address alone is not sufficient. The company must also consider the laws of the investor’s home jurisdiction, sanctions and AML requirements.

### 5.3 No general solicitation

For the Rule 506(b) tranche, the company must not:

- publish an offering page, teaser, “invest now” button or public form;
- send the same offer to a purchased list or an unrestricted social-media audience;
- speak at a public event about the offering in a way that invites investment;
- allow investors to forward the package to others without a documented introduction; or
- use the public TradingBible website, app, TV surface or social accounts to announce the round.

The existing investor-relations subscription list is not an offering list. A person who subscribed to company updates has not thereby been pre-qualified as an investor.

## 6. Regulation CF alternative

A Regulation CF offering may be considered only after the company has:

- selected a FINRA-registered funding portal or broker-dealer and completed the intermediary’s onboarding;
- prepared a Form C offering statement, including the required financial statements and disclosures;
- obtained SEC qualification of the offering statement;
- established escrow and investor-limit controls with the portal;
- adopted a communications policy that distinguishes pre-qualification education from a live offering; and
- accepted that the campaign will be public and that the company cannot control who sees it.

The company should not describe a Regulation CF campaign as “approved,” “live” or “open” until the SEC has qualified the offering statement and the portal has opened the campaign. A portal application, a draft Form C or a testing-the-waters communication is not qualification.

## 8. Communications rules

The following rules apply to every director, officer, employee, contractor and agent:

1. No offering communication may be sent from a personal account, a shared inbox or an unapproved marketing tool.
2. No communication may describe the company as “SEC approved,” “IPO ready,” “guaranteed,” “pre-vetted” or “safe.”
3. No projection, valuation, revenue figure or return scenario may be used unless it is in the approved package and clearly labelled as an assumption.
4. Every private-placement email must be one-to-one, identify the recipient, state that it is confidential and include the approved risk disclosure.
5. Every DocuSign envelope must be prepared from the controlled PDF and sent only to the verified signer.
6. Every investor question that could affect the offering terms must be routed to the CEO and counsel and recorded in the investor log.
7. No wire instructions may be sent in an email. Funding instructions must be delivered through a separately verified channel and confirmed by a call-back to a known number.

## 9. DocuSign and records

DocuSign will be used for completion fields and signature. The company must:

- verify the signer’s legal name, email, capacity and authority before sending;
- use the controlled PDF generated from the current document version;
- anchor tabs only to the signer’s own execution block and completion fields;
- retain the certificate of completion, the completed PDF and the envelope history;
- record any resend, correction, decline or void in the execution register; and
- treat a completed envelope as execution evidence, not as a regulatory approval or an admission that the offering is complete.
