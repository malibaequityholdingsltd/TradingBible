**Controlled template · Version 1.0 · 12 November 2026**

> **Controlled template — counsel review and local adoption required.** This document is a company policy or reference framework. It is not an executed agreement, a legal opinion, a regulatory approval, a payroll filing, an insurance policy or a promise of a benefit. Record adoption and approval references in the Workforce Compliance Register before treating it as operative. Local addenda control where they are more protective.

## 1. The controlling rule

A Delaware corporation is not automatically compliant with the employment, payroll, tax, insurance or workplace law of the country or state where a person actually works. The work location controls the local obligations. A remote employee in another state or country may create registrations, withholding, insurance, leave, data-protection and termination obligations that Delaware does not address.

This guide is a control framework, not a legal opinion. Confirm the current law with local counsel before an offer, a start date or a payroll run. Where local law is more protective than a company policy, local law controls.

## 2. Jurisdiction summaries

### United States

Federal rules apply nationwide, then the state and local rules where the employee actually works. Delaware incorporation does not replace state payroll, withholding, insurance or leave registrations.

**Employer responsibilities**

- Use the company EIN for employment-tax administration and keep the IRS notices.
- Complete employment-eligibility verification, including Form I-9, and retain the record.
- Collect Form W-4 and applicable state withholding elections.
- Withhold and remit federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare; pay the employer share.
- Handle federal unemployment tax and register for applicable state unemployment and disability programmes.
- Follow minimum-wage, overtime, working-time and recordkeeping rules.
- Provide required workplace notices and follow anti-discrimination, accommodation and anti-retaliation law.
- Confirm state-specific paid leave, workers’ compensation and disability requirements.

**Employee responsibilities**

- Provide the documentation required for Form I-9 within the lawful timeframe.
- Submit Form W-4 and applicable state withholding forms.
- Provide accurate payroll, tax and work-authorisation information.
- Record working hours accurately where required.
- Follow applicable federal, state and local workplace rules.

**Sources to verify:** IRS employer tax guidance · US Department of Labor wage and hour guidance · EEOC employer guidance.

**Caution:** State and local rules can be more protective than federal law. Confirm the law of the employee’s actual work location before the start date.

### Nigeria

A US company employing someone who works in Nigeria must establish an appropriate Nigerian employment and payroll arrangement. Delaware incorporation does not remove Nigerian employment, tax, pension, social-insurance or data-protection obligations.

**Employer responsibilities**

- Use a legally appropriate employment arrangement and issue written terms with the required particulars.
- Maintain employee and payroll records in accordance with Nigerian requirements.
- Operate PAYE deductions and remittances with the relevant tax authority.
- Assess whether the company and employee fall under the mandatory contributory pension regime and register where required.
- Assess NSITF registration and the Employees’ Compensation Scheme contribution.
- Meet applicable workplace safety and employee-compensation obligations.
- Follow Nigerian leave, working-hours and termination rules.
- Apply Nigerian data-protection requirements to employee records.

**Employee responsibilities**

- Provide accurate identity, tax and payroll information.
- Provide tax-identification and pension details where applicable.
- Provide a valid bank account for salary payment.
- Sign the employment contract and relevant company policies.
- Follow security, confidentiality and data-handling requirements.
- Complete required work and training.
- Report workplace incidents and compliance concerns.

**Sources to verify:** Pension Reform Act and National Pension Commission guidance · NSITF Employees’ Compensation Scheme guidance · Nigerian tax and data-protection guidance · Local employment counsel.

**Caution:** Pension coverage, contribution rates, NSITF contributions and written-particulars requirements depend on the current law and the employment arrangement. Confirm with Nigerian counsel before the first payroll run. The commonly stated pension contribution reference is 10% employer and 8% employee for covered employees; do not treat that as a conclusion for every worker.

### Other countries

Do not use a US or Nigerian template for another country. Engage local employment counsel and a payroll provider before making an offer or setting a start date.

**Employer responsibilities**

- Confirm the lawful employing entity or an employer-of-record arrangement.
- Obtain a local contract, mandatory particulars and language requirements.
- Confirm payroll, tax, social security, pension, insurance and workers’ compensation.
- Confirm working time, leave, probation, termination and works-council rules.
- Confirm data-protection, background-check and monitoring rules.

**Employee responsibilities**

- Provide the identity, right-to-work, tax and payroll documents required locally.
- Sign the local contract and policy acknowledgements.
- Complete mandatory local training and health-and-safety induction.

**Sources to verify:** Local employment counsel · Local payroll provider · Local regulator guidance.

**Caution:** This register does not provide a legal conclusion for any country outside the United States and Nigeria.

## 3. United States — state and local control

Federal law applies nationwide. The state and locality where the employee works may add or modify requirements, including:

- state income-tax withholding and unemployment-insurance registration;
- state disability insurance and paid-family-leave programmes;
- state minimum wage, overtime, meal-and-rest-break and pay-statement rules;
- state workers’ compensation coverage;
- state and local paid-sick-leave rules;
- state anti-discrimination, accommodation and background-check rules;
- state wage-payment, final-pay and deduction rules;
- state privacy, monitoring and AI-employment rules;
- local business licences and workplace notices.

Do not assume a Delaware registration, a federal EIN or a policy written for one state satisfies another state. Record the employee’s work state and county/locality in the workforce register. Confirm the state addendum before signature.

## 4. Nigeria — local employing arrangement

A US company employing a person who works in Nigeria must establish a lawful Nigerian employment and payroll arrangement. The options may include a Nigerian subsidiary, an employer-of-record arrangement or another structure confirmed by counsel. The structure affects contracts, PAYE, pension, NSITF, insurance, data protection and termination.

The company should confirm:

- written particulars and the local contract;
- PAYE registration, deductions and remittances;
- pension applicability and contributions;
- NSITF registration and the Employees’ Compensation Scheme;
- workplace safety and employee-compensation obligations;
- leave, working-hours and termination rules;
- Nigerian data-protection requirements for employee records.

The commonly stated pension reference for covered employees is 10% employer and 8% employee. That is not a conclusion for every worker or arrangement. Applicability, current rates and the approved pension arrangement must be confirmed before the first payroll run.

## 5. Other countries

Do not use a US or Nigerian template for another country. Engage local employment counsel and a payroll provider before the offer or start date. Confirm the lawful employing entity, contract language, mandatory particulars, payroll, tax, social security, pension, insurance, working time, leave, probation, termination, works-council and data-protection rules.

## 6. Cross-border hiring checklist

1. Record the actual work country, state/region and city.
2. Confirm the lawful employing entity or employer-of-record arrangement.
3. Confirm employee or contractor classification under local law.
4. Confirm payroll, tax, pension, social-insurance and insurance registrations.
5. Confirm the local contract and mandatory written particulars.
6. Confirm work authorisation and right-to-work verification.
7. Confirm leave, working time, rest periods and public holidays.
8. Confirm termination, notice, consultation and final-pay rules.
9. Confirm data-protection, monitoring, background-check and AI-employment rules.
10. Record the local counsel review and approval reference in the register.
