# TRADINGBIBLE HOLDINGS, INC. — EMPLOYEE HANDBOOK

**Controlled template · Version 1.0 · 12 November 2026**

> **Controlled template — counsel review and local adoption required.** This handbook is a company policy framework. It is not an executed contract and it is not legal advice. Employment counsel in each hiring jurisdiction must review it, and the company must record adoption in the Workforce Compliance Register before it is issued as an operative handbook. Local addenda override inconsistent global text. Nothing in this handbook promises equity, options, a bonus, health insurance, a pension, relocation or any other benefit unless a separate written plan or contract says so.

## 1. About this handbook

TradingBible Holdings, Inc. is a Delaware corporation that operates TradingBible. The company is pre-launch. It is not a licensed broker, investment adviser, custodian, exchange or fund manager, and no employee may present it as one unless the relevant activity is authorised and operational.

This handbook explains the standards expected of everyone who works for the company, including full-time employees, part-time employees, remote employees and individual contractors where the context applies. It does not replace an employment contract, a local addendum, a benefit plan or the law of the country or state where the person actually works.

The company may update this handbook. Material changes will be communicated in writing. A change does not reduce a right that local law makes mandatory. Where a local addendum conflicts with this handbook, the local addendum controls for that jurisdiction.

## 2. Employment status and classification

The company determines employment status under the law of the work location and the actual working relationship, not merely the label in a contract. A person who works under the company’s direction, on the company’s schedule, with company equipment and as part of the company’s operations is normally an employee, even if a document calls them a contractor.

Every employee should have:

- a written contract or statement of particulars;
- a job title, department and named manager;
- a work location and, where relevant, a state or region;
- a start date, pay rate and pay frequency;
- a classification (employee or contractor) and, in the United States, an exempt or non-exempt status where applicable;
- a written job description and decision authority;
- a probation or initial assessment period only where lawful;
- a route to raise concerns and request support.

Employees must not be asked to provide irrelevant medical, family, political, religious or financial information. Identity, immigration, payroll, medical and background-check records are collected only for legitimate purposes and kept in the controlled HR file.

## 3. Equal opportunity, anti-harassment and accommodation

The company is committed to a workplace free of unlawful discrimination, harassment and retaliation. Decisions about hiring, pay, promotion, training, discipline and termination must be based on job-related criteria and the needs of the business, not on a protected characteristic.

Harassment includes unwelcome conduct that creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment, including sexual harassment, unwanted advances, derogatory comments, threats and retaliation. It can happen in person or through email, chat, video calls, social media or AI-generated content.

Every employee must:

- treat colleagues, customers, partners and candidates professionally;
- report harassment, discrimination or retaliation to People Operations, Legal or the confidential reporting channel;
- cooperate with a company investigation;
- refrain from retaliation against anyone who reports a concern or participates in an investigation.

The company will review reports promptly and fairly. Where local law requires a works council, employee representative, specific investigation procedure or external reporting route, the local addendum will identify it. Medical information connected with an accommodation request is kept confidential and shared only with people who need it to assess the request.

## 4. Conduct and conflicts of interest

Employees must act honestly and in the company’s best interests. The following conduct is prohibited:

- fraud, theft, bribery, kickbacks or improper payments;
- falsifying records, invoices, time records, payroll data or security logs;
- unauthorised access to systems, data or accounts;
- sharing credentials or bypassing access controls;
- entering customer data, source code, credentials or material non-public information into unapproved AI tools;
- promising investment returns, guaranteed trading profits, guaranteed funded-account payouts or guaranteed investor returns;
- presenting an unapproved product, partnership, licence or regulatory status as real;
- using company funds or equipment for personal benefit without approval;
- discriminating, harassing or retaliating against anyone;
- concealing a conflict of interest or a suspected incident.

Employees must disclose outside business interests, directorships, paid advisory work, family relationships that could affect company decisions, and gifts or hospitality above the threshold in the gifts policy. Trading in a company security or a partner’s security while in possession of material non-public information is prohibited.

## 5. Confidentiality, intellectual property and data protection

Confidential information includes source code, product designs, research, models, customer data, financial records, investor materials, contracts, security information and anything the company marks or reasonably treats as confidential. Employees must use confidential information only for company work and must not disclose it to anyone outside the company without authorisation.

Every employee must sign the company’s confidentiality agreement and intellectual-property assignment. The assignment covers work created in the course of employment, including code, content, designs, inventions and improvements. Local employee-invention rules may require additional notices or a separate schedule; the local addendum will address them.

Personal data must be collected and used only for legitimate business purposes. Employees must follow the privacy notice, access controls and retention schedule. Do not copy customer or employee data to a personal drive, personal email, chat or unsecured device. Report a suspected data breach immediately.

## 6. Information security and AI

Employees must use company-approved devices, accounts, storage and communication tools. Multi-factor authentication is required for email, source control, cloud, payroll, banking, admin and password-manager accounts. Passwords must be unique and stored in the company password manager. Do not share credentials in chat or email.

The following are prohibited:

- installing unauthorised software or browser extensions on company devices;
- disabling endpoint protection, encryption or logging;
- connecting company devices to untrusted networks without approved secure access;
- storing company data in personal cloud accounts;
- using unapproved AI tools for customer data, source code, credentials or material non-public information;
- publishing AI-generated content without human review.

AI-generated output must be reviewed by a competent human before it is used in customer-facing, regulatory, financial or security-sensitive work. The company may monitor company systems and devices to the extent permitted by law. Monitoring will be described in the acceptable-use policy and any local notice.

## 7. Remote work, hours and attendance

The company is remote-first. Employees must have a reliable connection, a safe workspace and an approved device. Work hours, availability and time-zone expectations are set by the manager and the applicable local law. Employees must record hours accurately where required and must not work unrecorded overtime.

Remote work does not change the employee’s work location for tax, payroll, insurance or employment-law purposes. An employee who intends to change their work country or state must obtain written approval before the change. The company will assess payroll, tax, insurance, data-protection and immigration consequences before agreeing.

Employees must take rest breaks and statutory leave. Managers must not pressure employees to work while on leave or during protected rest periods.

## 8. Pay, expenses and benefits

The company pays salaries through an approved payroll provider or employer-of-record arrangement in the employee’s country and currency. Pay frequency, deductions, payslips and statutory contributions are set out in the local addendum and payroll record.

Employees must submit accurate expenses with receipts, a business purpose and the required approval. Personal expenses, fines and unsupported costs are not reimbursable. Expense fraud is misconduct.

Benefits are governed by the applicable plan documents and local law. The proposed benefit tiers in the Payroll and Benefits Framework are not entitlements. No employee should be promised equity, options, a bonus, health insurance, a pension, relocation or a similar benefit unless the board or an authorised officer has approved it in writing and the necessary plan documents exist.

## 9. Leave and time off

Employees receive the leave required by the law of their work location and any additional leave set out in their contract or local addendum. This may include annual leave, public holidays, sick leave, parental leave, bereavement leave, voting leave, military leave, disability-related leave or other statutory leave.

Employees must request leave through the approved process and provide the information the company reasonably needs. Medical information should be limited to what is necessary. The company will not retaliate against an employee for taking legally protected leave.

## 10. Performance, training and promotion

Every employee has a job description, a manager and measurable objectives. Managers should hold regular one-to-ones, give clear feedback and document material performance concerns. Performance reviews must be based on job-related criteria and should give the employee an opportunity to respond.

The company provides mandatory compliance, security and role training. Additional development is subject to the approved training budget and business needs. Promotion is not automatic and depends on the role, performance, available budget and approval.

## 11. Grievances, discipline and termination

Employees should raise concerns with their manager, People Operations, Legal or the confidential reporting channel. If the concern involves the manager, the employee may escalate to the next manager, People Operations or Legal. Retaliation is prohibited.

The company may use coaching, a written warning, a performance-improvement plan, suspension (where lawful) or termination. Serious misconduct may result in immediate termination where local law permits. The company will follow the procedure required by local law, including notice, consultation, works-council involvement and final pay.

Nothing in this handbook creates a fixed term of employment or a guarantee of continued employment. In the United States, employment is at-will unless a written contract or local law says otherwise. In Nigeria and other countries, the local contract and mandatory law control.

## 12. Health, safety and emergencies

Employees must follow the health-and-safety policy, complete the remote-work risk assessment and report accidents, injuries, security incidents and near misses. Employees must keep emergency contacts current and follow the company’s emergency instructions.

The company will maintain the insurance, workers’ compensation or equivalent coverage required in each work location. The existence of a policy does not replace the company’s duty to provide a safe working environment.

## 13. Company property and offboarding

Employees must use company property responsibly and return it on or before the exit date. This includes laptops, phones, security keys, ID cards, documents and access credentials. Employees must not retain company data after termination and must allow the company to verify that personal copies have been removed.

The company will disable access, recover property, calculate final pay and preserve records under the offboarding procedure. Where local law requires a different notice or consultation process, the local addendum and counsel-approved procedure control.

## 14. United States addendum

This section applies to employees whose work location is in the United States. Federal law applies nationwide, and the law of the state and locality where the employee works may add or modify obligations.

The company will:

- use its EIN for employment-tax administration;
- complete Form I-9 employment-eligibility verification within the lawful timeframe;
- collect Form W-4 and applicable state withholding elections;
- withhold and remit federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare, and pay the employer share;
- handle federal unemployment tax and applicable state unemployment and disability programmes;
- follow minimum-wage, overtime, working-time and recordkeeping rules;
- provide required workplace notices;
- follow applicable anti-discrimination, accommodation and anti-retaliation law;
- confirm state-specific paid leave, workers’ compensation and disability requirements.

Employees must provide accurate payroll and work-authorisation information, record hours accurately where required and follow applicable federal, state and local workplace rules. Delaware incorporation does not replace the law of the employee’s work state.

## 15. Nigeria addendum

This section applies to employees whose work location is in Nigeria. The company must use a legally appropriate employment arrangement and issue written terms with the required particulars.

The company will, subject to current law and local counsel confirmation:

- maintain Nigerian employment and payroll records;
- operate PAYE deductions and remittances with the relevant tax authority;
- assess whether the company and employee fall under the mandatory contributory pension regime and register where required;
- assess NSITF registration and the Employees’ Compensation Scheme contribution;
- meet applicable workplace safety and employee-compensation obligations;
- follow Nigerian leave, working-hours and termination rules;
- apply Nigerian data-protection requirements to employee records.

Employees must provide accurate identity, tax, pension and bank information through the approved secure channel, sign the local contract and policies, follow security and data-handling requirements, complete required training and report workplace incidents and compliance concerns.

The commonly stated pension reference for covered employees is 10% employer and 8% employee. That is not a conclusion for every worker or arrangement. Applicability and current rates must be confirmed with Nigerian counsel and the relevant regulator before the first payroll run.

## 16. Other countries

An employee who works outside the United States or Nigeria must not be placed on a US or Nigerian template by default. The company will engage local employment counsel and a payroll provider before the offer or start date, and will confirm the lawful employing entity, contract, payroll, tax, social security, insurance, working time, leave, termination and data-protection requirements.
