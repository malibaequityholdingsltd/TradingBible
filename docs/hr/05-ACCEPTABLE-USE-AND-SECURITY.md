# TRADINGBIBLE HOLDINGS, INC. — ACCEPTABLE USE AND INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY

**Controlled template · Version 1.0 · 12 November 2026**

> **Controlled template — counsel and security review required.** This policy is a company control framework. It is not legal advice and it does not replace the security standards required by a customer contract, regulator or insurer. Local law may require consultation, a works-council review, a privacy notice or a specific monitoring disclosure before it is adopted.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy protects TradingBible’s people, customers, systems, data and reputation. It applies to every employee, contractor, director, officer, intern and anyone else who accesses company systems or data. It covers company devices, personal devices used for company work, email, chat, source code, cloud services, AI tools, removable media and physical documents.

## 2. Account and access rules

1. Use a named company account. Do not use shared credentials or another person’s account.
2. Use the company password manager for every work credential. Do not reuse a personal password.
3. Enable multi-factor authentication for email, source control, cloud, payroll, banking, admin and password-manager accounts. Administrators should use a phishing-resistant factor where available.
4. Grant the minimum access needed for the role. Do not share admin passwords, API keys, tokens or certificates.
5. Do not bypass access controls, logging, endpoint protection or network restrictions.
6. Report a lost device, suspected phishing, unexpected MFA prompt or suspected compromise immediately.
7. Remove access on the same day a person leaves or changes role. Review privileged access monthly and all access quarterly.

## 3. Devices and remote work

Company-issued devices must have disk encryption, screen lock, endpoint protection and current patches. Personal devices may be used for company work only if the company has approved the device and the security controls. Do not store customer data, source code or confidential documents on an unmanaged personal device.

Remote work must use an approved secure connection. Do not connect a company device to an untrusted network without the approved secure-access arrangement. Lock the screen when leaving the device. Do not leave devices or documents visible in a public place.

## 4. Data handling

| Classification | Examples | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Published website content, public marketing | May be shared publicly after approval |
| Internal | Policies, plans, internal notes | Company-approved storage; need-to-know |
| Confidential | Source code, research, contracts, financial records | Encrypted storage; access controlled; no personal accounts |
| Restricted | Customer data, identity documents, payroll, credentials, material non-public information | Strict need-to-know; logged access; no AI tools without approval |

Do not copy restricted data to email, chat, personal drives, removable media or unapproved AI tools. Do not use production customer data in a test environment unless it has been de-identified or the use is approved and controlled.

## 5. Acceptable use

Company systems may be used for reasonable personal communication during breaks, provided the use is lawful, professional and does not interfere with work, security or performance. The following are prohibited:

- accessing, storing or sharing unlawful, harassing, discriminatory or exploitative content;
- using company systems for personal business, political campaigning or unauthorized commercial activity;
- installing unauthorized software, browser extensions or scripts;
- using company funds or accounts for personal benefit;
- attempting to access another person’s account or data without authorization;
- scraping, harvesting or bulk-exporting data without approval;
- circumventing a licence, access control or security measure;
- using company systems to compete with the company or misappropriate its information.

## 6. AI tools and generated content

Only approved AI tools may be used for company work. Do not enter customer data, employee data, credentials, source code, material non-public information or confidential research into an unapproved tool. Follow the approved-tool list and the data-handling rules for each tool.

AI-generated output must be reviewed by a competent human before it is used in customer-facing, regulatory, financial, security-sensitive or public content. Do not present AI output as verified fact, a professional opinion or a guarantee. The company may restrict or withdraw an AI tool at any time.

## 7. Email, chat and external communications

Use company email for company business. Do not forward company email to a personal inbox. Be alert to phishing, unexpected attachments, payment-change requests and impersonation. Verify a payment or bank-detail change through a separate known channel.

Do not make public statements, publish content or speak to the media on the company’s behalf unless authorized. Do not promise investment returns, guaranteed trading profits, guaranteed funded-account payouts or guaranteed investor returns. Employees must not present the company as a licensed broker, investment adviser, custodian, exchange or fund manager unless the relevant activity is authorized and operational.

## 8. Monitoring and privacy

The company may monitor company systems and devices to protect the company, customers and employees, to investigate misconduct or to comply with law. Monitoring will be proportionate and consistent with applicable law and any required notice or consultation. The company does not monitor personal accounts or personal devices except as authorized by law and the approved device policy.

Employees should have no expectation of privacy in company systems, company email or company devices, to the extent permitted by law. Personal data collected through monitoring will be handled under the employee privacy notice and retention schedule.

## 9. Incident reporting

Report the following immediately to the security team and the manager:

- lost or stolen device, security key or identity document;
- suspected phishing, malware or unauthorized access;
- accidental disclosure of customer, employee or confidential data;
- a misdirected email containing restricted data;
- a compromised password, API key or token;
- a suspected fraud, payment diversion or social-engineering attempt;
- a breach of this policy by anyone.

Do not investigate alone, delete evidence or contact the suspected attacker. Preserve logs and follow the incident-response instructions.

## 10. Return of property and access

On or before the exit date, return all devices, security keys, ID cards, documents and storage media. The company will disable accounts, revoke tokens, rotate shared secrets and verify that company data has been removed from personal systems. Do not retain company data after the engagement ends.

## 11. Consequences

A breach of this policy may lead to access restriction, coaching, a written warning, suspension, termination or contractor removal, and may be reported to law enforcement or a regulator where appropriate. The company will follow the applicable local process and will not retaliate against a person who reports a concern in good faith.

## 12. Policy changes and acknowledgement

The company may update this policy to address a change in systems, law, customer requirements or risk. Material changes will be communicated. The employee or contractor must sign a new acknowledgement when required.
