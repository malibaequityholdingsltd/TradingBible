# Security Awareness and Phishing

**Controlled programme document · management approval not recorded**

## 1. Objective

Employees are often targeted through phishing, impersonation and social engineering. The objective is not to blame people for every click. It is to build fast reporting, safe habits and a culture where people can challenge unusual requests without fear.

## 2. Non-negotiable employee rules

- Do not share passwords or authentication codes.
- Do not approve an MFA prompt you did not initiate.
- Do not click suspicious links or open unexpected attachments.
- Do not install unapproved applications or browser extensions.
- Do not use personal email or personal messaging for company business.
- Do not store company data in personal cloud storage.
- Do not upload customer data, source code or confidential material to an unapproved AI service.
- Do not send customer data through personal messaging accounts.
- Do not bypass security controls to meet a deadline.
- Report suspected compromise immediately.
- Report a lost or stolen device immediately.
- Use the company password manager.
- Lock your screen when leaving your device.
- Verify changes to bank details, payout instructions or supplier payment details through a separate known channel.

Executives follow exactly the same standards. A founder or CEO must not have an unrestricted, unmonitored account that bypasses the programme.

## 3. Onboarding training

Before access to customer data or production systems, each employee or contractor completes:

- acceptable use and information security;
- phishing and social engineering;
- passwords, MFA and hardware keys;
- device and data handling;
- incident reporting;
- AI and confidential-data rules;
- payment-fraud and impersonation awareness; and
- privacy and customer-data obligations.

Record completion, date, module version and assessment result.

## 4. Annual and role-based training

All staff complete annual refresher training. Additional role-based training is required for:

- administrators and engineers: secure development, access control, secrets and incident response;
- finance and payroll: payment fraud, bank-detail change, refunds and approval controls;
- support: account recovery, identity verification and social engineering;
- executives: crisis communication, legal notification and business-email compromise; and
- people managers: handling reports, privacy and respectful investigation.

## 5. Phishing simulations

- Run simulations at a reasonable frequency, at least annually and preferably quarterly.
- Include credential-harvesting, invoice-fraud, MFA-prompt and executive-impersonation scenarios.
- Do not use simulations to embarrass, punish or single out employees.
- Measure reporting rate, time to first report, click rate and credential-entry rate.
- Provide immediate education and a safe way to ask questions.
- Never simulate a real emergency in a way that could cause operational harm.
- Do not run a simulation during a live incident or a sensitive company event.

## 6. Reporting

A single reporting route must be visible and easy:

- security email or ticket form;
- direct escalation for a suspected active compromise; and
- an out-of-hours route for SEV1/SEV2 events.

The first responder thanks the reporter, preserves evidence, and does not forward suspicious attachments or links to colleagues. Reporters must not be penalised for good-faith reports.

## 7. Payment and impersonation controls

- Require callback verification to a known number for bank-detail changes.
- Do not rely on email alone to approve a payment or payout change.
- Use dual approval for high-value payments and refunds.
- Train finance to recognise urgent, secret or authority-pressure language.
- Verify supplier invoices against an existing purchase order and known contact.
- Treat a change in payment instructions as a high-risk event even if the email looks genuine.

## 8. AI and data handling

Employees must not enter customer data, source code, credentials, material non-public information or confidential documents into an unapproved AI tool. Use only the approved tool and data class. Require human review of AI output used in customer-facing, financial or compliance work.

## 9. Metrics

Track:

- training completion by role;
- phishing report rate and time to first report;
- repeat click and credential-entry rates;
- incident reports that came from employee reporting;
- help-desk recovery-verification failures; and
- completion of corrective training after an incident.

Do not use click rate alone as a performance judgement for an individual.

## 10. Evidence

Record training modules, completion dates, assessment results, simulation scenarios and aggregate results, reporting-channel tests, and corrective actions. Store personal training records in the restricted HR system, not in the security register; the register records aggregate completion and evidence references.
